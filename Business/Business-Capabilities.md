# Business Capabilities

## Purpose

This document defines the core business capabilities that the ARQA platform will enable and support. It provides a high-level, business-oriented view of what the organization needs to do to achieve its objectives, independent of how these capabilities are implemented technically.

## Scope

This document covers the essential business functions and competencies required for the ARQA platform to deliver value. It includes capabilities related to data acquisition, processing, analysis, reporting, and decision support within the ARQA domain.

## Principles

- **Modularity:** Business capabilities are defined as discrete, independent units that can be developed and evolved separately.
- **Reusability:** Capabilities are designed to be reusable across different business processes and platform components.
- **Business Alignment:** Each capability directly supports a specific business objective or strategic goal.
- **Measurability:** Capabilities are defined in a way that allows for the measurement of their effectiveness and contribution to business outcomes.

## Standards

All business capability documentation adheres to the standards outlined in the main [README.md](../../README.md) of this repository and the [Business README.md](../README.md).

## Design Decisions

- Business capabilities will be modeled using a hierarchical structure, breaking down high-level capabilities into more granular sub-capabilities.
- A capability map will be maintained to visualize the relationships and dependencies between different business capabilities.
- Each capability will be associated with specific business processes and value streams.

## Best Practices

- Engage business architects and subject matter experts to accurately define and validate capabilities.
- Regularly review and update the business capability model to reflect changes in business strategy and market conditions.
- Use a common language and taxonomy for defining capabilities across the organization.

## Risks

- **Incomplete Definition:** Failure to identify all necessary business capabilities leading to gaps in platform functionality.
- **Overlapping Capabilities:** Redundant capabilities leading to inefficiencies and increased development costs.
- **Lack of Prioritization:** Inability to prioritize capability development based on business value and strategic importance.

## Acceptance Criteria

- All critical business functions are represented by defined business capabilities.
- Business stakeholders agree on the scope and definition of each capability.
- The capability model provides a clear basis for architectural design and solution development.
